Track any company, product or topic across worldwide online news and Hacker News, and see whether coverage is rising or falling.

Give it search terms. It returns every matching article in your time window as one clean, deduplicated table, plus a coverage trend for each term. News covers outlets in 100+ languages and most countries, so it works for emerging markets as well as the US and Europe.

Each row also has url, language, comments and discussionUrl (Hacker News) and fetchedAt.

The SUMMARY record in the key-value store gives, per term: article counts, a news-volume timeline, and changePercent, the change in coverage between the first and second half of the window (e.g. "Nvidia: +144% over 7 days").

How to use

  1. Add Search terms: company names, products, topics. Multi-word terms match as a phrase.
  2. Pick a Time window (last hour to last 90 days).
  3. Optional: limit news to a language (e.g. hindi) or source country (e.g. india, nigeria, brazil).
  4. Run, or schedule it daily on Apify to get a fresh feed and trend each morning.

Use cases

  • Competitor and brand monitoring.
  • Market and investment research, including emerging markets.
  • Trend spotting: which topics are gaining coverage.
  • A news feed for AI agents and dashboards.

Pricing

$0.01 per search term plus $1 per 1,000 articles returned. Example: 5 terms with 50 articles each costs $0.30. Platform usage is included.

Note: the worldwide news source limits how fast requests can be made, so runs take about a minute per search term. This is deliberate: we stay within the source's published limits.

For AI agents

Call it through the Apify API or the Apify MCP server with {"queries": ["Stripe"], "timespan": "7d", "maxArticlesPerQuery": 20}. Every row has the same fields, and the SUMMARY record gives a one-number trend per term.

Data and responsibility

News articles come from the GDELT Project's free public API (gdeltproject.org), which indexes online news worldwide; Hacker News stories come from its official public search API. We return headlines, links and metadata, not full article text, and no author or user data. We follow each source's rate limits.
